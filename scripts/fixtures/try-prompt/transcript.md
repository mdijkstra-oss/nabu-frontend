# Town hall on the riverside redevelopment

Transcript | 3 March 2026 | Community centre, main hall

**MODERATOR (Priya Nair):** Good evening, everyone. We have three members of the planning committee with us tonight, and the developer's representative. I'll ask each of them for a short opening statement, and then we'll take questions from the floor. Please keep your questions brief so we can get to as many people as possible.

**Councillor Tom Okafor:** Thank you, Priya. I want to start by saying that nobody on the committee is pretending this is a simple decision. The riverside site has been derelict for eleven years, and every proposal we have seen in that time has fallen apart on the question of flood risk. This one is the first that has come with an independent hydrological survey attached, and I think that deserves to be taken seriously even by people who don't like the design. I'll say more about the affordable housing quota later, because I know that's what most of you came here to talk about.

**Dr. Helen Marsh:** I'm the committee's environmental advisor, so I'll keep to my patch. The survey Tom mentioned models a one-in-two-hundred-year flood event and finds the ground floor of the eastern block would be under forty centimetres of water. The developer's answer is to raise the whole eastern block by a metre and put parking underneath. That is a legitimate engineering solution, but I want people to understand what it means for the riverside path — it becomes a wall for about ninety metres. I have asked for a revised drawing that shows the path from a pedestrian's eye level, and I have not yet had one.

**Priya Nair:** Thank you, Helen. Mr. Castellanos, you're representing Northbank Developments.

**Rafael Castellanos:** Yes, and thank you for having me. I'll be direct: we heard the criticism of the eastern block at the last consultation and we have taken it on board. The revised scheme, which I brought copies of tonight, drops the parking podium and moves the car park to the western end of the site, where the ground is already higher. The path stays at grade for its full length. I accept that the earlier drawings were not good enough on this point, and I'd rather say so than defend them.

**Tom Okafor:** Can I come in on that? Because that is a substantial change and the committee has not seen it. If the podium is gone, the flood modelling that Helen described no longer applies to the scheme in front of us. I'm not saying that's a bad thing, but we can't vote on a survey of a building that isn't being built any more.

**Helen Marsh:** That's right. I would need to see the revised model before I could advise either way.

**Rafael Castellanos:** Understood. The revised hydrology is with the consultants now and I expect it back within three weeks.

**Priya Nair:** Let's take a first question. The gentleman in the second row.

**Resident (Mr. Bhatt):** Sanjay Bhatt, I live on Wharf Lane. My question is for the councillor. You mentioned the affordable housing quota. The last time this came up, the figure was thirty per cent and it quietly became twenty. What is it tonight, and who decides if it changes again?

**Tom Okafor:** It is twenty-five per cent in the application as submitted, and I will be honest with you, Mr. Bhatt, that is lower than I would like. The mechanism for changing it is a viability assessment, which the developer commissions and the council can challenge. In the last scheme we didn't challenge it, and I think that was a mistake. I have already written to the chief planning officer to say that if the number moves this time, I want the assessment published in full before the committee sits.

**Rafael Castellanos:** For the record, we have no plans to revise the quota downward. Twenty-five is the number we intend to build.

**Priya Nair:** The lady near the door.

**Resident (Ms. Adebayo):** Funmi Adebayo. This is for Dr. Marsh. You said the path becomes a wall for ninety metres in the old plan. In the new plan, with the car park at the west end — is that not simply moving the wall to where the allotments are?

**Helen Marsh:** It's a fair challenge and I don't have the drawings to answer it properly. What I can say is that the western end is on higher ground, so a car park there doesn't need to be raised in the same way, and there is no reason in principle why it has to present a blank wall to the allotments. But I'd rather look at the plans than reassure you on principle.

**Rafael Castellanos:** The western car park is single-storey and screened by a planted bank on the allotment side. I'll make sure Dr. Marsh has the elevations tomorrow.

**Priya Nair:** Thank you. We have time for two more. The young man at the back.

**Resident (Owen Price):** Owen Price, I'm a student and I rent on Mill Street. Nobody has said anything about what happens to the people who rent in the buildings on the edge of the site. Are they being bought out or just moved on?

**Tom Okafor:** Three of the buildings on the northern edge are within the red line and their tenants have been served notice. That's forty-one households. The council has a duty to rehouse anyone in a council tenancy, which is nineteen of them. For the private tenants there is no such duty and I'm not going to pretend there is. What I have asked the developer for is a written commitment that those households get first refusal on the affordable units at a rent no higher than they pay now.

**Rafael Castellanos:** We have agreed that in principle. It will be in the section 106 agreement.

**Owen Price:** In principle isn't a lease.

**Rafael Castellanos:** No, it isn't. When it's in the agreement it will be binding, and I'll come back here and say so.

**Priya Nair:** Last question. The gentleman by the pillar.

**Resident (Mr. Kowalski):** Piotr Kowalski, I run the boatyard. Nobody has mentioned the slipway. If the path stays at grade the whole length, where does the slipway go?

**Helen Marsh:** It stays where it is. The path crosses it on a lifting section, which is in the original drawings and I don't believe has changed. Mr. Castellanos?

**Rafael Castellanos:** It hasn't changed. The slipway is retained and the boatyard's access is protected in the plans.

**Piotr Kowalski:** Good. That's all I wanted to hear.

**Priya Nair:** Then we'll close there. Thank you all for coming, and thank you to the panel. The revised drawings and the new hydrology report will be published on the council's website as soon as the committee has them, and there will be a further session before any vote.

## Second half: breakout on transport and the school

**Priya Nair:** We said we'd close, but the committee has offered another forty minutes for the two topics that came up most in the written submissions: traffic on Wharf Lane, and the primary school's capacity. Councillor Okafor, you wanted to start with the traffic survey.

**Tom Okafor:** I did. The survey counted vehicles on Wharf Lane over two weeks in January. On a weekday morning between eight and nine it carries about four hundred and twenty vehicles, most of them through-traffic heading for the ring road. The developer's transport statement predicts the scheme adds a hundred and ten movements in that hour. That is a quarter more, on a street that already backs up to the junction. I want that on the record before anyone tells me it is marginal.

**Rafael Castellanos:** I won't call it marginal, and I won't hide behind the statement either. The number is right. What the statement also says, and what I'd ask people to weigh, is that half of those movements are displaced from the existing surface car park at the retail units, which closes when the scheme opens. Net, the increase on Wharf Lane is closer to fifty movements. That is still an increase, and the section 106 includes a contribution to a signalised junction at the ring road that the highways team has wanted for years.

**Dr. Helen Marsh:** Can I add one thing on transport, because it touches my brief? The riverside path is meant to carry cycles as well as pedestrians. If it stays at grade for its full length, as Mr. Castellanos now says, it becomes the first continuous cycle route from the station to the retail park. That is not in the transport statement at all, and I think it should be, because it changes the picture on the lane.

**Priya Nair:** Let's take a question on this. The woman in the green coat.

**Resident (Ms. Lindqvist):** Astrid Lindqvist, Wharf Lane, number fourteen. My children walk to school along that lane. Fifty more cars or a hundred and ten, I want to know whether the crossing by the chemist gets a light. It's been promised twice.

**Tom Okafor:** It has, and both times the money fell through with the scheme it was attached to. That's exactly why I want the junction contribution in the legal agreement and not in a letter of comfort. If this scheme goes ahead, the crossing is built before the first flat is occupied. I'll put that in writing tonight.

**Rafael Castellanos:** We'd accept that as a condition. I'll say so to the case officer tomorrow.

**Astrid Lindqvist:** Then I'll hold you both to it.

**Priya Nair:** Now the school. Councillor Okafor, you have the numbers.

**Tom Okafor:** Riverside Primary has three hundred and fifteen pupils on a roll of three hundred and thirty. The education team's model says a scheme of this size brings roughly forty-five primary-age children over five years. That is thirty more than the school can take without expansion. There is a contribution in the draft agreement for two additional classrooms, and the head has said the site can accommodate them on the field. What she cannot accommodate is a bulge class in year one before the classrooms exist, so timing matters more than money here.

**Rafael Castellanos:** The phasing has the family-sized units in the second phase, which is year three onwards, precisely so the classrooms come first. I know that sounds convenient. It is also in the drawings, and I'm happy for it to be conditioned.

**Resident (Mr. Nakamura):** Kenji Nakamura, I'm a governor at the school. I want to correct one thing gently. The field can take the classrooms, yes, but it is also the only outdoor space the school has, and two classrooms take about a fifth of it. The head has said the site can accommodate them; she has not said she'd be glad of it. If there is any way to put the classrooms on the northern edge, against the boundary wall, the loss is much smaller.

**Helen Marsh:** That's a design question I can pick up with the education team. The northern edge is where the drainage runs, so it isn't free, but I'd rather look at it than assume.

**Priya Nair:** Thank you, Mr. Nakamura. One more on the school. The gentleman with the pushchair.

**Resident (Mr. Adeyemi):** Bola Adeyemi. Two questions, quickly. First, if the classrooms are built and the scheme then stalls, who pays to run them? Second, is there any nursery provision at all in the scheme, because there is none within a mile.

**Tom Okafor:** On the first, the classrooms are funded by the contribution and staffed by the school on the basis of the roll, so if the children don't come the school isn't obliged to open them. On the second, there is a shell unit on the ground floor of the western block that the agreement reserves for a nursery operator at a peppercorn rent for ten years. Whether an operator takes it is not something the council can force, but the space is there.

**Rafael Castellanos:** We've had two operators express interest. Nothing signed, and I won't pretend otherwise.

**Priya Nair:** Dr. Marsh, you wanted to close on the ecology survey.

**Helen Marsh:** Briefly. The survey found a bat roost in the old warehouse on the eastern edge, which is one of the buildings coming down. That is not unusual and it is not a reason to refuse, but it means the demolition has to be licensed and it has to happen outside the roosting season, which is May to August. The developer's programme has demolition in June. That has to move, and I want to be sure the whole timeline moves with it rather than being squeezed.

**Rafael Castellanos:** It will move to September. I hadn't seen the survey result before tonight, so I'm saying that without having checked it against the rest of the programme, but the roost isn't negotiable and I know it.

**Piotr Kowalski:** Sorry, one more from me — the boatyard's crane is in the same building. Does the licence affect when I can move it out?

**Helen Marsh:** It affects the demolition, not your access. You can move the crane whenever suits you; it's the roof coming off that has to wait.

**Piotr Kowalski:** Understood.

**Priya Nair:** Then that really is everything. Thank you for staying. Councillor Okafor will circulate a note of the commitments made tonight — the crossing, the classroom phasing, the demolition date — with the revised drawings, and there will be a further session before the committee votes. Good night.

## Addendum: written questions answered on the night

The moderator read out the written questions submitted before the meeting and the panel answered them in turn.

**Priya Nair:** The first written question comes from Margaret Osei, who could not be here. She asks whether the warehouse facade on the eastern edge, which is locally listed, will be kept, and if not, why the heritage statement calls its loss "less than substantial harm".

**Rafael Castellanos:** The facade is kept. The heritage statement was written against the earlier scheme, where the podium meant the ground floor had to be rebuilt behind it, and the phrase Mrs. Osei quotes came from that version. With the podium gone, the facade stays as it is, brick and lintels and the loading doors, and the statement will be rewritten to say so. I'd rather withdraw the phrase than defend it.

**Tom Okafor:** For the record, the conservation officer has told me the same, so I'm content on that one.

**Priya Nair:** The second is from Daniel Ferreira on Mill Street. He asks about construction hours and whether piling will happen at weekends.

**Rafael Castellanos:** Standard hours, eight to six on weekdays and eight to one on Saturdays, no Sundays. Piling is weekdays only and it is a continuous flight auger rig, which is the quieter kind. That is a condition we'd expect and would accept without argument. There will be a phone number for the site manager on the hoarding from day one, and Mr. Ferreira can have my direct line if he'd like it.

**Dr. Helen Marsh:** I'd add that the piling method also matters for the river wall. The augered piles put less vibration into the ground than driven ones, which is the reason the Environment Agency has not objected. If the method changed, that consent would need to be revisited, so it is not just about noise.

**Priya Nair:** The third is from Grace Whitmore, who runs the cafe on the corner. She asks whether the hoarding will block her frontage during construction and for how long.

**Rafael Castellanos:** The hoarding line runs behind her frontage, not across it. There is a period of about six weeks when the pavement outside is narrowed for the crane base, and we would put a covered walkway there so the cafe stays reachable. I've spoken to Mrs. Whitmore before tonight and I think she is content, but she should say so herself rather than have me say it for her.

**Priya Nair:** Grace Whitmore is here, in fact.

**Grace Whitmore:** I am. Six weeks I can live with. What I asked in writing, and haven't heard, is whether the workers will be told they can use the cafe. Last time there was a site on this road the contractor put a canteen in a cabin and I lost a year.

**Rafael Castellanos:** There will be no canteen cabin. I'll put that in the construction management plan.

**Grace Whitmore:** Then I'm content.

**Priya Nair:** The fourth is from Samuel Achebe, who asks a technical question for Dr. Marsh: what return period the drainage is designed to, and where the water goes in a storm larger than that.

**Dr. Helen Marsh:** The drainage is designed to a one-in-a-hundred-year storm with a forty per cent allowance for climate change, which is what the current guidance requires. Beyond that, the exceedance route is across the riverside path and into the river, which is the right direction for it to go — the path is designed to flood before any building does. I have asked for the exceedance route to be drawn rather than described, and that request stands.

**Tom Okafor:** And I'll say, since Mr. Achebe asks a question that goes to whether the committee understands what it is voting on, that the drainage strategy is one of the documents I want redrawn against the revised scheme before we sit, for the reason Dr. Marsh gave earlier: the podium is gone, and the drawings that assumed it are no longer of the scheme in front of us.

**Priya Nair:** The last written question is from Yusuf Karimi, who asks simply: what happens if the committee refuses.

**Tom Okafor:** Then the applicant can appeal, and an inspector decides instead of us. I'll be honest about the arithmetic. This site has been allocated for housing in the local plan since 2019, and refusing an allocated site is only defensible on grounds the plan did not foresee. Flood risk was foreseen; the plan required exactly the survey we now have. Traffic was foreseen. What was not foreseen is the loss of the private tenancies on the northern edge, and that is why I keep coming back to it. If we refuse, I would want it to be on that ground and no other, because that is the ground on which I think we would win.

**Rafael Castellanos:** I'll only say that we would prefer to build something this room can live with than win an appeal against it. That is not a threat and it is not a plea; it is a description of what is cheaper for us.

**Priya Nair:** Thank you both. That closes the written questions and the meeting. The note of commitments Councillor Okafor mentioned will include tonight's answers on the facade, the construction hours, the cafe, the drainage and the tenancies. Good night, and thank you for staying so late.

## Corrections and follow-ups received after the meeting

The following were submitted to the clerk within the week and are appended to the record at the panel's request.

**Councillor Tom Okafor (by letter, 5 March):** I said on the night that Riverside Primary has three hundred and fifteen pupils on a roll of three hundred and thirty. The head has since corrected me: the roll is three hundred and fifteen and the number on it is three hundred and eight. The point stands, and if anything is slightly stronger, since the headroom is seven pupils rather than fifteen. I also want to correct the impression I may have given that the council refused to challenge the last viability assessment. We did not refuse; we did not think to. That is a worse answer and I would rather it be the accurate one.

**Dr. Helen Marsh (by email, 6 March):** Two follow-ups. First, I have now seen the western elevations that Mr. Castellanos promised, and the planted bank on the allotment side is real: it is a metre and a half high at its lowest and the car park roof sits below the top of it from the allotment path. I am satisfied on that point. Second, I have spoken to the education team about the northern edge of the school field, as Mr. Nakamura suggested. The drain runs closer to the wall than I thought and the classrooms cannot go there without diverting it, which is possible but would eat most of the contribution. I have asked for a costed option so the governors can decide with numbers rather than with my guess.

**Rafael Castellanos (by letter, 6 March):** As promised, the revised hydrology has come back from the consultants earlier than expected. Without the podium, the eastern block's ground floor is above the modelled one-in-two-hundred level by thirty centimetres rather than below it by forty, which is the outcome the whole redesign was for. The report is with the case officer and with Dr. Marsh. On demolition, the programme has moved to the second week of September to clear the roosting season, and the crane at the boatyard can be moved out at Mr. Kowalski's convenience before then; I have written to him separately. On the nursery unit, one of the two operators has now signed a heads of terms. It is not a lease, as Mr. Price would rightly say, but it is more than an expression of interest.

**Kenji Nakamura (by email, 7 March):** Thank you to Dr. Marsh for taking the northern-edge question seriously. The governors met on Thursday and would rather lose a fifth of the field than half the contribution, if that is the choice, but we would like to see the costed option before we say so formally. We would also ask that whichever option is chosen, the classrooms are built in the summer holiday and not in term, which the phasing seems to allow.

**Owen Price (by email, 7 March):** I asked on the night whether the tenants on the northern edge were being bought out or moved on, and I was told there would be a written commitment on first refusal for the affordable units. I have now seen the draft clause and it says first refusal "subject to eligibility criteria to be agreed". I would like the record to show that "to be agreed" is doing a great deal of work in that sentence, and that nineteen households have been asked to trust it. I do not doubt anyone's good faith. I doubt the sentence.

**Astrid Lindqvist (by telephone, noted by the clerk, 8 March):** Ms. Lindqvist asked that the record show she has received a letter from Councillor Okafor confirming the crossing by the chemist will be a condition of any consent, and that she is grateful, and that she will still hold him to it.

**Priya Nair (moderator's note, 9 March):** The panel has agreed that the next session will be held once the revised drawings, the new hydrology, the redrawn drainage strategy and the costed classroom option are all published, and not before. A date will be set when the last of those is received. My thanks to everyone who wrote in.
