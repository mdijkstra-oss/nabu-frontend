# On the shape of a description

The question of how an archive should be organised has never had a single answer, and the reason is that it is not one thing but a negotiation between what a collection happens to contain, what the people who built it thought mattered at the time, and what a reader arriving thirty years later is actually trying to find out. Say it plainly: an archive is never a neutral record, and the compromise most institutions arrive at, which is to record a great deal and expose only a little, works well enough right up until somebody asks a question that runs across the grain of the description. Anyone who has spent an afternoon with an archive of any size knows the feeling of finding exactly the detail they needed recorded three times over in three incompatible ways, while the one fact the whole afternoon depended on was left to be inferred from a date and a surname.
