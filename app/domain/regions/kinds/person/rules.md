# Person

A person region is a stretch of a document that is one person's: their words, their
act, or their account as the document records it. Not what is written about them —
what the document gives them.

## Where an occurrence sits

An occurrence is the place the document names the person a passage belongs to. The
naming has to be in the text; nothing outside it counts.

Ask of a passage: whose is this, and does the document say? Where it does, and the
answer is a person, that naming is an occurrence.

Documents do this in whatever way suits them. A transcript labels turns. Minutes
name a person and a reporting verb, or record an act with no words at all. A
complaints log gives each entry a field for whoever complained. A letter signs off at
the end. A clinical note attributes an account to whoever gave it. These are not the
forms to look for — they are five documents doing one thing five ways. A document you
have not seen before will do it a sixth way, and that counts too.

The quote is the words that name the person, as the document writes them. Take the
name and the marker that carries it when they are adjacent (`Rutte:`), and the name
alone when they are not. A title belongs with its name: "Mrs. Okafor" is one
occurrence, never two.

## What is never an occurrence

- A pronoun. "He continued" keeps the current person's region going and is not a new
  occurrence.
- A mention inside someone else's passage. "We asked Rutte about it", in the
  interviewer's own turn, names Rutte and gives him nothing. The passage stays the
  interviewer's.
- A name with no passage. "Present: Mrs. Sharma (chair), Mr. Bell…" records who was
  in the room and gives none of them anything to own. However many people it names,
  it produces no occurrences.
- A description of a person. A biography names its subject on every page, and the
  subject owns none of it — the words are the biographer's. What someone said, did or
  reported is theirs; an account of them written by somebody else is not.

## The value

The value is one person, in a form short enough to be an identifier: lowercase, no
titles, no punctuation. `rutte`, not `President Rutte` or `Mr. Rutte`. Keep the rest
of what the document gives, initials included — `f hanley`, so that a later
`j hanley` stays a different person. A person the document never names takes the role
it gives them: `interviewer`, `respondent`, `complainant`.

The same person must get the same value everywhere, so reuse a value from the list
you are given whenever the occurrence is that person, even when the document spells
the name differently this time. Coin a new value only when nobody on the list is this
person.

## How far a region reaches

A region is every sentence the person owns — everything the document records as
theirs — and it stops at the last one. It does not stop at the next name, which
belongs to the next region. Sentences that carry on with a pronoun — "She went on to
recommend…" — still belong to the person last named; the naming switching to a
pronoun does not end the region.

A recorded act reaches only the sentences that record it: "Seconded by Ms. Delgado.
Carried." is Delgado's, and nothing more. An entry in a log or a register reaches the
whole entry, because all of it is that person's record.

Where the naming trails the passage — "This is great, said Rutte", or a letter signed
at the end — the region starts before the naming and takes it in. A person can own a
whole document this way.

Regions of two people may overlap. An act or a brief interjection inside someone's
turn belongs to its own person without ending the surrounding region, and a passage
the document gives to two people jointly — "Mr. and Mrs. Given reported…" — sits in a
region of each.

Stage directions, timestamps and notes that sit inside a passage stay inside it. A
heading that introduces a new section does not belong to the person before it.
