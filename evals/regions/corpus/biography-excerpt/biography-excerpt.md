# Chapter four: the Bellwether years

Hanley arrived in the town in the spring of 1974 with no work and nowhere to
live, and within three years had built the practice that would occupy the rest
of his career. The early commissions were small and mostly domestic. What
distinguished them was an unwillingness to accept a brief as given.

His contemporaries found him difficult. One partner, interviewed decades later,
said only that he had been impossible to argue with and usually right.

Hanley himself was less generous about the period. In a letter to his sister,
written in November 1979, he set it out plainly:

> The work is not the problem. The work is the only part of it I understand. It
> is everything around the work that I cannot get the measure of, and I am
> beginning to think I never will.

He did not send the letter. It was found in his papers after his death and is
quoted here for the first time.
